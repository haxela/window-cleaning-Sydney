Is It Worth Paying a Professional Instead of Doing It Yourself?

Often yes, but not always. If you have a small flat with a few windows you can reach, a bucket, a squeegee and a free Saturday will do the job.

The trouble starts with upper floors, wide sliding doors and glass you cannot reach safely. Ladder falls are a real risk, and an injury costs far more than a cleaning quote.

Doing a two-storey home properly, inside and out, can eat a whole weekend. A trained crew has the tools and routine, so they finish faster. They also notice things you might miss, like gritty tracks that scratch glass.

Ask yourself three things. Can I reach every window safely? Do I own the right tools? Is my weekend worth more than the quote? If any answer is no, hiring help makes sense.

How Professionals Get Streak-Free, Spot-Free Glass

Two methods do most of the work. The first is the squeegee and scrim. The cleaner wets the glass with a soapy mix and scrubs it with a scrim, which is a soft cloth sleeve. Then they pull a squeegee across in one smooth stroke and wipe the edges. The skill is in the stroke. A bad one leaves lines. A good one leaves nothing.

The second is the water-fed pole system. It is a long pole with a soft brush on top, and clean water runs up through it onto the glass. That water is deionised, which means the minerals are taken out. Minerals cause the white spots you see when normal water dries. With none left, the glass dries clear on its own. This method suits high outside windows because the cleaner stays on the ground.

Timing counts as well. Hard, direct sun dries the soap too fast and leaves streaks. Experienced cleaners often start on the shady side of a house and follow the shade around.

What a Standard Window Cleaning Service Usually Includes

Every company writes its quote a little differently, so read the scope before you say yes. A typical home service can include the items below when they are agreed up front.

  • Interior and exterior glass: one side or both, depending on what you book.
  • Frames and sills: wiped to lift dust and dirt around the glass.
  • Window tracks: loose grit and debris cleared from the channels.
  • Fly screens: brushed or washed to remove dust, pollen and cobwebs.
  • Sliding and glass doors: fingerprints and pet marks removed.
  • Glass balustrades and pool fence glass: cleaned when they appear in the job.

Common Window Problems Caused by Coastal Weather and Pollution

Glass can look perfect for a day, then the weather gets to work. Homes near the coast collect salt spray, which dries into a hazy film. Homes near busy roads get a grey layer of traffic dust. Rain mixes with that dust and leaves spots as it dries.

Then come the small things. Birds leave droppings, and the longer they sit, the harder they are to lift. Spiders hang cobwebs in every corner of the frame. If a builder or a neighbour has been working close by, fine construction dust sticks to the glass. It needs a gentle rinse first so it does not scratch.

Most of this comes off in a normal clean. One problem does not: hard water stains. These are mineral marks, often from sprinklers, and they bond to the glass.

What a Standard Clean Does Not Cover

A fair quote tells you what it leaves out. A standard clean does not usually include heavy mineral deposits, hard water stains, glass restoration, skylight cleaning or a full post-construction clean. Each of these needs a closer look first. Some marks will not lift with normal cleaning, and rubbing harder can scratch the glass.

If your windows have cloudy patches that did not budge when you tried, tell the cleaner before the visit. They can inspect the glass and say honestly if it can improve. But sometimes the damage sits in the glass itself, and no cleaner should promise otherwise.

Ask about extras too. Gutter cleaning and pressure washing are separate jobs, and they get their own quotes.

How Single-Storey, Double-Storey and Multi-Storey Properties Are Handled

A single-storey home is the easiest. Most windows sit within reach of a pole or a short ladder, so the crew moves fast.

Double-storey homes need more planning. Upper windows may sit above a roof, a pool or a steep side path. The crew decides how to reach each one safely before they start. Some need a ladder on firm, level ground.

Working at heights has rules for a reason. A responsible company follows work health and safety (WHS) rules and writes a Safe Work Method Statement for risky jobs. That is a simple plan listing the hazards and how the crew will control them. If a company cannot explain how it handles upper floors, keep looking.

Taller buildings, like strata blocks and high-rise towers, are a different job. They can call for rope access and specialist gear, so they go through a dedicated commercial team.

What Affects the Cost of Window Cleaning

Window cleaning cost comes down to five things: how many windows you have, how many floors, how easy they are to reach, how dirty the glass is, and what you want included. Cleaning both sides costs more than one.

For a rough idea, our general guide runs from about $180 to $350 or more for a single-storey home. A double-storey home runs from about $330 to $550 or more. These are estimates, not fixed prices. A small flat with six windows and a big house with a pool fence are different jobs.

Be careful with cheap quotes. A low price can mean the quote skips the tracks, skips the outside, or skips insurance. Ask what the price covers before you compare numbers. The cheapest quote only saves money when it covers the same work.

How Often Should You Book a Window Clean?

It depends on where you live and what your glass faces. A home near the coast or a busy road may need a clean every few months. A home on a quiet, tree-lined street may do fine with two a year.

A regular window cleaning schedule keeps the buildup light, so each visit goes faster and the glass stays clearer. Pick a monthly or quarterly rhythm and stick with it.

A one-off clean makes sense at certain moments. Book one before you list your home for sale, because clear glass makes rooms look brighter in photos. Book one when you move out of a rental, since the final inspection often includes the windows. And book one after a renovation, once the dust has settled.

How to Choose a Trusted Window Cleaning Company

When you shop for professional window cleaning, reviews help, but they only tell part of the story. Use this short checklist to compare local window cleaners.

  • Insurance: ask for proof of public liability cover, and check the amount.
  • Safety: look for police-checked staff and a clear plan for upper floors.
  • Written scope: the quote should name the windows, the sides and any extras.
  • Method: ask how they reach high glass and whether they use pure water.
  • Real reviews: read a few that describe the job, not just five stars.
  • Communication: they should answer your questions clearly before you book.
  • Red flag: a price far below everyone else, with no details to back it up.

Window Cleaning FAQs

Is it worth getting windows professionally cleaned?
Yes, for most homes. It pays off most when you have upper floors, large glass or little spare time. For a small flat with easy windows, doing it yourself can work fine.

What is a reasonable price for window cleaning?
It depends on the size and access of your home. As a general guide, many single-storey homes fall between about $180 and $350 or more, and double-storey homes between about $330 and $550 or more. Ask for a written quote that lists what is included.

How much does it cost to clean all your windows?
The cost follows the window count, the floors, the glass condition and the scope. Both sides, tracks and fly screens add to the price.

How often should windows be cleaned?
Most homes do well with a few cleans a year. Coastal homes and homes near busy roads may need cleaning more often.

Can I book inside and outside window cleaning in one visit?
Yes. You can book the inside, the outside or both.
